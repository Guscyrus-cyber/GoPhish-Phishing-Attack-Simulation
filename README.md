# GoPhish-Phishing-Attack-Simulation
In this project, I used GoPhish, an open-source phishing framework, to improve my penetration testing skills and simulate phishing attacks. GoPhish allows security professionals to conduct realistic phishing campaigns by mimicking common social engineering tactics, such as fake login pages and malicious email templates. By using GoPhish, I was able to create a convincing phishing email that appeared to come from a trusted source (in this case, Twitter), tricking users into believing their account was compromised and urging them to enter their login credentials on a fake website.

The tool operates by setting up a phishing campaign with customizable email templates and landing pages. Once the email is sent, the victim is directed to the fake landing page, where their credentials are captured if they enter their login details. GoPhish enables you to track user interactions, such as who clicked on the link or submitted their credentials, giving insights into how effective the phishing attempt was. By using GoPhish for testing, I was able to simulate an actual phishing attack in a controlled environment, helping to assess vulnerabilities and raise awareness about social engineering attacks. This exercise contributes to enhancing security measures and training individuals on recognizing and responding to phishing attempts.

This phishing email targets users by pretending to be a legitimate communication from Twitter, claiming that the user's account has been compromised. It uses urgency and fear tactics by warning that the account has been locked due to suspicious activity and urging the recipient to click a link to "secure their account." The link redirects the user to a fake Twitter login page, where they are asked to enter their credentials, allowing the attacker to steal their login information. This type of social engineering attack preys on the recipient’s trust and fear of losing access to their account, making it effective if the user does not carefully scrutinize the email.

To recognize this email as a fake, users should first look for suspicious signs such as an unusual sender address (in this case, an unrelated email like "abertkooshano@gmail.com" rather than Twitter's official domain), poor grammar or spelling mistakes, and unprofessional language. A legitimate company like Twitter would never ask for account verification via an unsolicited email. Users should also check the URL of any links before clicking on them, ensuring they point to the legitimate website. To remedy this, users should avoid clicking on links in unsolicited emails, especially those asking for personal information, and instead visit the official website directly by typing the URL into their browser. Enabling two-factor authentication (2FA) on accounts provides an additional layer of security in case login credentials are compromised.
